FRIDAY BRIEF • SEPTEMBER 18, 2026 DA 🇩🇰

Fed Defies the White House: Rate Hike, AI Capex Drain, and Term Premium Expansion

Weekly Movement: The Federal Reserve and newly appointed Chair Kevin Warsh raised the policy rate by 25 bps to 3.75–4.00%, defying explicit public calls from Trump for easing toward 1.0%. The US 10-year yield consolidates near 5.0% (4.97%), with the 30-year reaching 5.29%.
Underlying Transmission: High energy baselines (Brent > $97) collide with aggressive debt issuance by hyperscalers funding the AI arms race, absorbing vast global liquidity pools. Simultaneously, NBIM's benchmark reduction (-$80B) removes an indispensable sovereign anchor buyer.
Perspective: With public debt topping $40 trillion and annual interest costs exceeding $1 trillion, the zero-cost liquidity era has closed. Expanding term premia compel supply chains and long-term contract architectures to structurally reprice duration risk.
FRIDAY BRIEF • SEPTEMBER 11, 2026

Norway's Oil Fund Trims Sovereign Bonds: The Anchor Weighs In

Weekly Movement: Norges Bank Investment Management (NBIM, $2.3T) formally recommended lowering its sovereign bond allocation benchmark from 70% to 50%, translating to a structural divestment of ~$75–80B in government paper.
Underlying Transmission: This retreat lands amid a heavy US auction schedule. The Treasury's $6B buyback program failed to compress yields, leaving the 10-year yield pressured upward toward 4.97%.
Perspective: Sovereign allocators are reallocating away from nominal paper into real assets. The resulting supply shock leaves major deficit nations without unconditional balance sheet absorbers.
FRIDAY BRIEF • SEPTEMBER 4, 2026

Peg Under Pressure: Danmarks Nationalbank Deploys FX Reserves

Weekly Movement: Danmarks Nationalbank intervened with -5.6B DKK to defend the krone peg, testing the persistent policy discount (-0.40%) against the ECB.
Underlying Transmission: Global dollar liquidity tightening and cross-border portfolio shifts continue to put peripheral European pegs on the defensive.
Perspective: The Danish yield discount relative to Frankfurt faces structural exhaustion if foreign exchange outflows persist.
